# Evidence Selection and Sentence Progression

## Observed Failure

The five-paper abstract demonstration exposed a failure not solved by factual
coverage: several measurements perform the same rhetorical job, consecutive
sentences list results without advancing a question, and familiar academic
wording does not repair the missing argument. This is user-observed development
feedback, not a newly measured preference result.

The old instructions already advise decisive evidence and coherent explanation.
The operational ambiguity is that "preserve numbers" can be read as "include
every fact". The correction distinguishes an admissible evidence pool from the
selected argument. It adds no universal number cap, sentence formula or new
handoff schema. Opposing evidence and distinct co-contributions remain binding.

## Admitted Extension

Eight `ai.pattern.*` cards extend the existing `ai-spec` catalog to version 0.5.
The 130 pinned imported cards, including 78 sentence patterns, are unchanged.
Local patterns overlap in wording purpose but add a different decision contract:
the question answered, preceding and following functions, and non-transfer.
They reuse existing lookup filters and `ai_spec_ids` in role packets.

- `bottleneck-choice`: why this operation responds to this obstacle.
- `operation-effect`: what an operation changes, not just its module name.
- `contribution-pair`: how complementary contributions form a usable system.
- `result-answer`: which comparison answers the experimental question.
- `distinct-evidence`: when additional measurements change the interpretation.
- `scope-transfer`: which axis is held out and what adaptation is absent.
- `family-contrast`: a supported positioning axis, not a paper-by-paper list.
- `result-boundary`: a condition that changes a finding, not a generic caveat tail.

All templates and positive/negative examples are local MIT editorial synthesis.
Source papers retain their rights. The examples are synthetic, not measurements
from the cited papers. There is no imported executable content, provider call,
credential access, manuscript upload or automatic semantic scorer.

## Application and Evaluation Boundary

The owning guides now specify: group evidence by reader question; choose the
decisive comparison; draft the dependency between statements; then retrieve
wording and verify the final prose against the full evidence pool. The proposer,
writer and reviewer reuse their existing fields. Preservation applies to the
bindings of selected numbers, not compulsory coverage of every fact.

The default `argument-transfer.md` remains byte-identical to its frozen baseline.
Full regressions caught two archive-protection failures when the progression
passage was initially placed there. The passage was moved unchanged to the
functional-pattern owner, `writing-library.md`, and its links/card owners were
updated instead. No archive assertion, raw draft or historical gate was changed.
The generation receipt retains the pre-relocation hashes and discloses this
post-generation metadata/guidance relocation; it does not pretend the live files
were frozen or silently regenerate the writer's output.

The [five-paper follow-up](../../evals/abstract-argument/README.md) preserves the
old outputs and displays author summaries, old drafts and new candidates. All
five are development cases now. Changed length budgets, prior familiarity and
source-derived guide observations rule out a fresh blind-gain claim. A fresh
writer context does not prove absence of pretraining familiarity or enforce
tool isolation. Independent source/translation reading is recorded separately
from any prose preference.

Catalog tests check complete retrieval, conditions, provenance, bad boundaries
and packet integration. HTML/browser checks concern rendering and navigation.
Neither demonstrates writing improvement. Frozen previous failed experiments
remain unchanged; their votes are not pooled with these developmental examples.

Independent source reading found that the raw Sparsh draft inherited a task
ranking from the paper's prose that conflicts with Table 10. The host removed
the disputed textile comparison and false count precision in a separately stored
correction; the raw draft and failure remain public. A later source-conflict check
was added to abstract selection, explicitly after generation. This case shows
why packet fidelity alone is not scientific correctness, not that the new guide
has passed a writing-quality test.
