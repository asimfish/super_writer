Baseline

Training loss at a single parameter vector can conceal differences in test performance among minima. Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) instead minimizes worst-case loss in a weight neighborhood with weight regularization, favoring solutions whose nearby parameters also have low loss. A PAC-Bayesian bound motivates this objective when Gaussian perturbations do not reduce population loss and the parameter norm is at least one; it does not certify the practical approximation. SAM perturbs weights along the gradient and updates using the perturbed-weight gradient, dropping second-order terms and requiring two backpropagations. On CIFAR-10/100, gains over baselines allowed up to twice as many epochs support benefits beyond longer pointwise training: AutoAugment WideResNet reduces CIFAR-10 error from 2.2% to 1.6%. ImageNet training and pretrained EfficientNet finetuning also improve. Clean-test accuracy improves under random label corruption, but SAM trails MentorMix; bootstrapping narrows the gap without eliminating it at the highest noise rate. Complementary analyses find lower Hessian curvature and show that separate neighborhood maximizations on smaller subsets improve SAM generalization and make m-sharpness more predictive of generalization gaps in the studied settings. These diagnostics support neighborhood-based optimization without establishing curvature as the isolated cause, and an augmented SVHN configuration worsens.
