Baseline

PPO's critic predicts an infinite-horizon discounted return, but a reset does not always mean that future return is zero. Failure or goal completion ends the task trajectory and can be anticipated by the critic; a time limit instead interrupts a continuing trajectory. Because episode time is absent from observations, the critic cannot anticipate that interruption. Treating it as terminal would remove continuation value for a reason unavailable to the critic. The environment therefore distinguishes time-outs and augments their rewards with the critic's discounted prediction of future return, bootstrapping the target for the interrupted trajectory rather than the episode begun after reset. This distinction remains necessary with short rollouts: the 20-second episode limit is independent of the number of steps collected per policy update, and environments are not reset after every update. Episodes can consequently span multiple batches, so time-outs cannot be assumed absent or restricted to the final collection step.
