MISO

Initial draft 100 words

MISO is a neural SLAM back-end that represents an environment with multiresolution submaps. It uses pretrained encoders to initialize finer-level features from residuals left by coarser levels, then optimizes features and robot poses together. A second stage aligns submaps using their features and applies SDF refinement before fusion. The method was tested on ScanNet, FastCaMo-Large, and Newer College. On ScanNet scene 0011, alignment took 10.53 seconds versus 59.23 seconds for MIPS, starting with errors of 5 degrees and 0.20 meters. The results show faster optimization with competitive accuracy. Dense grids can require substantial memory in larger environments.

Doppler Correspondence

Initial draft 96 words

This paper introduces Doppler Correspondence for matching scans from 4D radar and LiDAR. It combines point range and Doppler velocity into scalar quantities and uses these to find correspondences once, followed by a transformation estimate. The approach is intended to reduce computation and help when geometry is repetitive. Tests cover sparse and semi-dense radar data and dense LiDAR data. On Campus2, transformation estimation takes 0.16 ms per frame, compared with 5.9 ms for unseeded point-to-point ICP. Accuracy is not always better, particularly during sharp turns. The paper also combines Doppler correspondences with ICP.

We introduce Doppler Correspondence, a geometrically and kinematically derived range–Doppler relation for associating consecutive 4D radar/LiDAR scans under a short-scan approximation. Doppler-informed scalar matching reduces dependence on repetitive geometry; spatially gated matches are computed once before standard rigid registration. On Campus2, transformation estimation excluding preprocessing takes 0.16 ms/frame versus 5.9 for unseeded point-to-point ICP. Accuracy is not consistently better, and the approximation degrades during sharp turns. Doppler 4D-ICP extends the method by retaining Doppler-derived matches while iteratively updating geometric matches.

We introduce Doppler Correspondence, a geometrically and kinematically derived relation for associating consecutive 4D radar/LiDAR point clouds. Under approximately constant velocities and small inter-scan rotation, corresponding points have approximately equal scalar scores computed independently from range and radial Doppler velocity. Thus, measured motion guides association before rigid registration, reducing reliance on repetitive geometry rather than changing the registration objective. A lightweight baseline matches scores once, rejects candidates using spatial and score thresholds, and fits a rigid transformation without iterative correspondence updates. Across radar and LiDAR evaluations, Campus2 transformation estimation takes 0.16 ms/frame versus 5.9 ms for unseeded point-to-point ICP, excluding preprocessing. Accuracy gains are not uniform: sharp turns can invalidate the approximation and cause drift. Doppler 4D-ICP extends the approach by retaining Doppler-derived matches while iteratively updating geometric matches within a weighted robust registration objective.

Building Rome with Convex Optimization

Initial draft 97 words

This paper presents XM for bundle adjustment and structure from motion. Learned depth turns image matches into 3D measurements, and per-frame scales adjust these measurements for depth prediction errors. XM estimates camera poses and landmarks using a convex relaxation, low-rank optimization, and a GPU solver. The SfM pipeline also includes filtering and CERES refinement. Experiments use bundle adjustment benchmarks and real image collections. On BAL-1934, solver time is 2.09 seconds for XM and 236.46 seconds for MANOPT on the same problem. The approach is faster in these tests, although depth quality and outliers remain concerns.

XM couples certifiable scaled bundle adjustment with scalable optimization. Learned depth lifts matched keypoints into 3D, with per-frame scales accommodating depth error during joint pose and landmark estimation. An empirically tight semidefinite relaxation supplies optimality bounds; a low-rank Riemannian staircase tests dual certificates to certify SDP solutions or increase rank and escape local minima. A C++/CUDA trust-region implementation solves BAL-1934 in 2.09 seconds versus MANOPT's 236.46 on identical lifted observations, excluding preprocessing. XM-SfM filters outliers and initializes CERES refinement of the original reprojection objective.

We present XM, a certifiable scaled bundle-adjustment solver that jointly estimates camera poses, landmarks, and per-frame depth scales. Learned depth lifts matched image points into camera-frame 3D measurements; frame scales adjust these measurements before alignment with global landmarks. V-HOP

Initial draft 97 words

V-HOP is a system for tracking the 6D pose of objects during manipulation. It uses RGB-D images and haptic feedback, with a point cloud that includes gripper shape, contact information, and an object pose hypothesis. A transformer combines visual and haptic features, building on FoundationPose. The model is trained on synthetic data from several grippers and tested on new objects and embodiments. On FeelSight-Occlusion, V-HOP reports 1.46 mm ADD-S error at 32 frames per second, compared with NeuralFeels at 2.14 mm and 3 frames per second. Real robot experiments also examine tracking and manipulation tasks.
