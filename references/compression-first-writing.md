# Explanation-Preserving Compression

Excellent scientific prose is selective, not exhaustive. A revision should make
the reader's most important answer easier to recover under the same evidence and
a declared information budget. More detail, more caveats, and more protocol
labels are not improvements by themselves.

The legacy filename remains for existing links. The six-paper compression pilot
failed its abstract gate: economy improved while explanations deteriorated. These
revised decisions are an engineering response, not a measured quality gain.

## Decide the argument before cutting

Write one target answer before diagnosing style: what should a reader remember
after this section? Choose at most three priority criteria for one pass. Other
criteria may be assessed and explicitly deferred; they must not force unrelated
sentences into the draft. Distinguish the main contribution, an indispensable
co-contribution, and supporting details before selecting evidence. Two coupled
bottlenecks can require two contributions; choosing the largest score first can
erase that structure. Do not invent a second contribution or a literature gap.

Identify at most three indispensable meaning relations, grounded in author
evidence, in the existing plan. Describe the operation, its object/direction and
the distinction the reader needs; a module name or intended benefit alone is
not an explanation. These are meanings to preserve, not extra sentences to append.
For instance, "cross-predicts actions" hides a reciprocal relation that "predicts
arm actions from hand actions and conversely" explains. This synthetic contrast
does not establish any particular paper's method or benefit.

## Allocate space by explanatory value

Build a connected argument: let the concrete obstacle motivate the distinctive
choice, explain the choice, then use decisive evidence to answer the contribution.
Choose another order when it makes the same relations clearer. Do not force a
five-sentence template, causal claim, numeric result or component inventory.

Trade repeated setup, module labels and per-task tallies for the needed relation.
Merge shared roles; move implementation prerequisites out of the abstract unless
they change the claim. Scope a positive result in its noun/setting rather than
interrupting each step with a disclaimer. Keep contrary evidence and conditions
whose omission changes the finding. An explanation of design intent is not
experimental proof of why it works.

Expand an underexplained sentence when necessary and within the agreed budget.
Prefer an informative verb and explicit object over stacked nouns, compressed
labels or extra transitions. Read it without the plan: can the intended reader
recover the relationship, rather than merely recognize its vocabulary?

## Budget and acceptance

Use the author's or verified venue's hard limits; otherwise agree a provisional
editorial budget. Do not infer that the current draft's length is a quality target.
In schema v3, `max_delta_words: null` uses the absolute word cap without a relative
cap; `0` still means an explicit no-expansion constraint. Never silently override
an explicit limit. Compare every attempt with the original, not its last revision.

The reviewer first reads the prose, then checks the facts and original:

- Can the reader recover the contribution and indispensable relationships?
- Does the evidence support those relationships, conditions and claim strength?
- Were the original's clear referents and other strengths preserved, including
  criteria outside this pass's priorities?
- Does each sentence advance the argument enough to justify its space?

A pre-existing lower-priority gap may remain; a newly introduced gap cannot be
deferred. Keep the original on a tie. Neither a shorter passage nor a longer,
more complete one wins automatically. Prefer the better explanation per unit of
attention within the actual constraints, with a located reason.
